# Customer proof story

Customer proof is the content tech buyers trust most and the content marketing teams produce least, because getting it approved is painful. This skill makes the story worth approving and makes the approval easy: clear structure, nothing invented, every number traceable.

## Two entry points

**No interview yet?** Write a customer interview guide using `references/customer-interview.md`. Ask who the customer is, what they use, and roughly what changed for them, then tailor 10–12 questions.

**Have raw material?** Follow the workflow.

## Workflow

### 1. Extract the story

From the source, pull out:

- **Before:** the situation, the pain, what they were using, what it was costing them (time, money, risk, morale)
- **Trigger:** why they looked for something new *then*
- **Decision:** what they evaluated and why they chose this product (this is gold for sales)
- **Implementation:** how long it took, who was involved, what was harder or easier than expected
- **After:** what changed, with numbers where the customer gave them
- **What's next:** expansion plans, what they'd tell a peer

### 2. Build the metrics ledger

Every number in the story goes in a ledger before it goes in the draft:

| Metric | Value | Exact source (quote, timestamp, or doc) | Customer-stated or derived? |
|---|---|---|---|

Rules:

- Use only numbers the customer actually said or provided. Don't calculate a percentage from two numbers unless you show the math and flag it as derived.
- Don't round up. "About 40%" stays "about 40%," not "over 40%."
- If the story has no metrics, it can still work as a qualitative story. Don't fabricate one, and don't pressure the story into a fake ROI frame. Flag `[METRIC NEEDED: e.g., hours saved per release]` and suggest the follow-up question to ask.

### 3. Write the assets

Structures and lengths are in `references/story-formats.md`.

1. **Full case study** — 600–1,000 words: challenge, why they chose us, implementation, results, what's next
2. **Short proof snippet** — 75–100 words for web pages and decks
3. **Pull quotes** — 3–5, edited lightly for clarity only, never for meaning
4. **Sales summary** — industry, size, use case, competitor displaced, key result, and when reps should use this story

### 4. Approval package

Customers approve faster when you make it easy. Produce:

- A list of every quote attributed to the customer, as it appears in the draft, with the original wording beside any lightly edited quote
- The metrics ledger
- Any details that might be sensitive for them (security tooling, vendors replaced, internal team names)
- A short, friendly approval request email to the customer champion

## Writing guidance

- The customer is the hero; the product is the tool. Stories where the vendor is the hero read like ads.
- Specifics beat superlatives. "Their two-person platform team now supports 180 developers" beats "dramatically improved efficiency."
- Keep the technical detail that a peer at a similar company would want: stack, scale, integration points.
- Lightly clean quotes (remove "um," fix grammar) but never put words in the customer's mouth.

## Anonymous stories

If the customer can't be named, describe them precisely enough to stay credible: "a top-10 U.S. regional bank with 2,000 developers" works; "a large enterprise" doesn't. Confirm the description doesn't make them identifiable if they've asked not to be.
