# Outlet formats

## Company engineering blog

- 1,200–2,500 words
- Can include code, architecture diagrams (described), and real internal numbers if approved
- Product mention is fine when it's how the team actually solved the problem — but the post should be useful to a reader who never buys
- Byline with role; short author bio at the end

## LinkedIn article or long post

- Post: 150–300 words. Article: 600–1,000 words.
- Personal and first-person. Opens with a line that earns the "see more" click.
- No product pitch; at most a light mention in the author's bio context
- End with a genuine question to the reader only if the expert would actually want the answers

## Trade publication byline

Examples: The New Stack, InfoWorld, DevOps.com, Dark Reading, CSO Online, SD Times, Federal News Network (for B2G topics).

- 800–1,500 words; check the specific outlet's contributor guidelines
- Strictly vendor-neutral: no company product names, no "at [Company], we built..." used as promotion. Editors cut it or reject the piece.
- A clear, arguable thesis in the first two paragraphs
- Practical takeaways readers can use regardless of vendor
- Include a 3–4 sentence pitch email for the editor: the angle, why now, why this author, word count
