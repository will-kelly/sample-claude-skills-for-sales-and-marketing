# Writing landmine questions

A landmine is a discovery question that helps the buyer evaluate something important — and happens to be where we're strong and the competitor is weak.

## What makes a good one

- **It's a question a smart buyer should ask anyway.** If the buyer's own architect would nod at it, it's good.
- **It's specific to their situation.** Tie it to scale, compliance, a migration, a team structure.
- **It never names the competitor.** The buyer connects the dots themselves, which is far more persuasive.
- **It can be checked.** The buyer can go ask the competitor, read the docs, or run a test.

## Patterns that work for technical products

- **Scale:** "What happens to pricing and performance when you go from 50 to 500 repos?"
- **Day-two operations:** "Who on your team will own upgrades, and how often do they happen?"
- **Security and compliance:** "How will you show auditors where this data is processed?"
- **Integration depth:** "Is that a native integration or a webhook you'll maintain?"
- **Lock-in and exit:** "If you moved off this in two years, how would you export your configs?"
- **Total cost:** "Beyond licenses, what does it cost in people-hours to run?"

## What to avoid

- Questions with a false premise
- "Did you know..." questions that are really attacks
- Questions about weaknesses the competitor fixed last quarter — check the changelog
