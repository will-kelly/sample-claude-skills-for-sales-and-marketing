# Positioning framework template

Use this structure. Sections in order. Keep each section tight; the "why it matters" lives in the persona section, not repeated everywhere.

---

## [Product name] positioning and messaging framework

**Version / date / owner**

### Positioning statement

One paragraph, internal only. Not a tagline.

> For [specific buyer] who [specific situation or pain], [product] is a [category frame] that [primary differentiated value]. Unlike [primary competitive alternative], we [key differentiator, stated concretely].

### Category frame

- **Category:** existing / subcategory / new
- **Why this frame:** 1–2 sentences

### Competitive alternatives

| Alternative | Type (direct / bundled / build / do nothing) | Where it wins | Where we win |
|---|---|---|---|

### Differentiated capabilities

| Capability | What it lets the customer do | Alternatives that lack it |
|---|---|---|

Three to five rows. If you have more than five, some of them are parity.

### Value pillars

Three pillars, each with:

- **Pillar name** (2–4 words, plain language)
- **Headline claim** (one sentence a buyer could test)
- **Supporting points** (2–3 bullets, specific)
- **Proof** (named source or `[PROOF NEEDED: ...]`)

### Persona messaging

One block per priority persona (3–4 max):

- **Persona:** title and what they're measured on
- **Their trigger:** what's happening when they start looking
- **Core message:** one sentence
- **What they need to believe:** 2–3 bullets
- **Proof that lands for them:** the type of evidence this persona trusts
- **Words to avoid with them**

### Messaging hierarchy

- **Tagline options:** 3, each under 8 words
- **Elevator pitch:** 2 sentences
- **Boilerplate:** 50 words, suitable for press releases

### Objections and responses

Top 3–5 objections a technical buyer raises, each with a short, honest response. Include at least one about the bundled or build-it-yourself alternative.

### Open questions

Proof gaps and assumptions the team needs to validate.
