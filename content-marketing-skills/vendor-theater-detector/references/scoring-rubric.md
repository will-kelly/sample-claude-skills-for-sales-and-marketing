# Scoring rubric

Five dimensions, 0 to 4 each. Higher is better (less theater). Total out of 20.

## 1. Claim honesty

- **0** Multiple absolutes that are plainly false (eliminate, zero, never, complete)
- **1** One or two absolutes in prominent positions
- **2** Absolutes softened but still unscoped
- **3** Claims scoped to what the product controls, minor slips
- **4** Every claim scoped; limits acknowledged where obvious

## 2. Mechanism clarity

- **0** No idea how it works after reading
- **1** Buzzwords stand in for mechanism ("AI-powered", "intelligent")
- **2** Mechanism hinted at but vague
- **3** Reader could explain roughly what it does to a colleague
- **4** Reader knows what it does, where it runs, and what they still have to do

## 3. Proof quality

- **0** No proof, or only logos
- **1** Unsourced numbers
- **2** Sourced numbers without method, or customer quotes from executives only
- **3** Sourced numbers with method, or a practitioner quote naming a real problem
- **4** Reproducible evidence (benchmark method, public demo, open data) or multiple practitioner voices

## 4. Reader fit

- **0** Written for the wrong reader entirely
- **1** Mixed signals: developer CTA with CISO language
- **2** Right reader, wrong pain point
- **3** Right reader, right pain, some generic filler
- **4** Reads like it was written by someone who has done the reader's job

## 5. Trade-off honesty

- **0** Product has no costs, setup, or limits according to the copy
- **1** Setup or cost hidden behind "easy" or "seamless"
- **2** Limits implied but not stated
- **3** At least one real limit or requirement stated plainly
- **4** Trade-offs named and framed as a reason to trust the rest

## Bands

- **16–20** Ships. Practitioners will argue with it, which is good.
- **11–15** Fixable. Do the top three rewrites and it ships.
- **6–10** Theater. Practitioners will bounce. Needs a product conversation, not an edit.
- **0–5** Start over from a user interview.
