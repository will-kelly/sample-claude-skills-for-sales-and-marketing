# ICP reality check

A lot of ICPs are written by people who've never met the customer. They pull from analyst reports, competitor websites, and a workshop where everyone agreed. The result reads fine and describes nobody. Content built on it misses, and the team concludes the content was bad.

This skill doesn't rewrite the ICP. It can't. Only customer contact can fix it. It finds the parts that look invented, says why, and hands back the questions that would replace guesses with evidence.

## When to use this

- Someone shares an ICP, persona, buyer journey map, or messaging framework
- Content performance is weak and the team suspects the audience is wrong
- A new marketing lead inherits an ICP and wants to know how much to trust it

## When not to use this

- Writing an ICP from scratch with no customer input. Say plainly that the output would be the exact problem this skill detects, and offer the interview guide in `references/evidence-questions.md` instead.
- Market sizing or TAM work

## Workflow

### 1. Ask where it came from

Before reading closely, ask (or note that it's unknown):

- Who wrote it, and when?
- How many customer or prospect conversations fed into it?
- Has sales or support reviewed it?

The answer changes how hard to look. "Built from 30 win/loss interviews" earns more benefit of the doubt than "from a workshop."

### 2. Scan for invention signals

Load `references/invention-signals.md`. Go section by section. Quote each line that matches a signal and name the signal.

### 3. Score confidence per section

For each major section of the ICP (firmographics, role, pains, triggers, objections, buying process, channels), give a confidence rating:

- **Evidenced** — specific detail that could only come from contact (a quote, a named tool, a real objection in the buyer's words)
- **Plausible** — reasonable but generic; could be true, unverified
- **Invented** — reads like it came from a template, an analyst report or wishful thinking

Explain each rating with a quoted line.

### 4. Turn gaps into questions

For every Plausible or Invented section, write two or three questions from `references/evidence-questions.md`, adapted to their market. Say who to ask: sales, support, customer success, a current customer, or a lost deal.

### 5. Deliver

1. **Provenance note** (what you know about where the ICP came from)
2. **Section confidence table** (section, rating, quoted line, why)
3. **Invention signals** (quote, signal, why it matters for content)
4. **Questions to replace guesses** (grouped by who to ask)
5. **Content risk** — one paragraph on what content built on this ICP is likely to get wrong
6. **The one section to fix first,** usually triggers or objections, because those drive what content gets read

## Voice

Honest without being smug. The person sharing the ICP may have written it under deadline with no customer access. The goal is a better ICP, not a verdict on them.
