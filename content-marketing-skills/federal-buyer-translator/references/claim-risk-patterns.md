# Claim risk patterns

Patterns that get commercial copy stopped in federal review. Match against these line by line.

## Compliance overstatement

| Pattern | Why it fails | Safe version |
|---|---|---|
| "FedRAMP compliant" / "FedRAMP certified" | FedRAMP authorizes; it doesn't certify. "Compliant" with no level or stage tells a reviewer the seller doesn't know the difference. | State the impact level and the actual stage: Ready, In Process, or Authorized. |
| "Meets NIST requirements" | NIST publishes many frameworks. Which one, which revision, which baseline, and who assessed it? | Name the framework and the evidence ("assessed against NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 Moderate baseline by a 3PAO"). |
| "Government-grade security" | Means nothing. Reads as a substitute for an authorization. | Delete, or replace with the specific authorization or control inheritance. |
| "Zero trust compliant" | There's no compliance regime by that name. Agencies map to zero trust strategies and maturity models. | Say which pillars or capabilities the product supports, and stop there. |
| "Ready for CUI" | CUI handling depends on the system boundary and the agency, not the product alone. | "Can be deployed within a boundary authorized for CUI" only if true, with the authorization named. |
| "Section 508 compliant" with no VPAT | Accessibility claims get checked. | Reference the current Accessibility Conformance Report (VPAT) and its date. |

## Unverifiable outcomes

| Pattern | Why it fails | Safe version |
|---|---|---|
| "Cut costs by 40%" with a commercial source | A contracting officer can't use a commercial customer's ROI to justify a federal buy, and may treat it as puffery. | Source it, scope it, or move it to a commercial case study link. |
| "Deploy in days" | ATO timelines dominate. Deployment speed without authorization speed is irrelevant. | Separate technical deployment time from authorization effort, and say what you do to shorten the second. |
| "Trusted by leading agencies" | If you can't name them, a reviewer assumes you can't. | Name agencies only with permission; otherwise use "[NEED: approved public sector reference]". |

## Procurement mismatch

| Pattern | Why it fails | Safe version |
|---|---|---|
| "Start your free trial" / "Sign up in minutes" | Most federal users can't click-through accept commercial terms. Free offers raise gift and ethics questions. | "Request an evaluation" or "Talk to our federal team about a pilot." |
| "Buy now" / credit card pricing | Wrong buying motion. | Point to the vehicle: "Available through [vehicle]" only if true. |
| "Limited-time pricing" | Federal buys don't move on your quarter. Reads as pressure. | Delete. |
| Per-user SaaS pricing with auto-renewal language | Conflicts with anti-deficiency rules and fiscal-year funding. | Leave pricing to the vehicle listing; don't describe renewal mechanics in marketing. |

## Commercial-only proof

| Pattern | Why it fails | Safe version |
|---|---|---|
| Fortune 500 logo walls | Signals "we sell to enterprises," not "we understand your mission." | Public sector references, or none plus a placeholder. |
| Growth or revenue language ("accelerate growth", "win more customers") | Agencies don't have customers in that sense, and they don't grow revenue. | Mission throughput, service delivery, backlog, citizen experience, audit findings closed. |
| Competitive-advantage framing | Agencies aren't competing. | Delete or reframe as capability gap closed. |
