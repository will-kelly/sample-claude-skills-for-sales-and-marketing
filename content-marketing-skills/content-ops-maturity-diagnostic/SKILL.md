# Content ops maturity diagnostic

"We need more content" is almost never the problem. Usually the team can't get a draft approved, can't find what it already published, or is producing for a buyer nobody's validated. More content just makes those problems bigger.

This skill finds the actual constraint. It interviews, scores six dimensions, and refuses to hand back a list of twenty recommendations. It names one bottleneck and the next two moves.

## When to use this

- A content, marketing or docs lead wants to know why output is slow, inconsistent, or not working
- Someone is about to hire writers, buy a tool, or adopt AI to "fix content" and wants a check first
- A leader needs a defensible view of where the team stands before planning

## When not to use this

- SEO-only audits (use an SEO skill)
- Single-asset feedback (use an editing skill)
- Teams of one with no process yet. Say that maturity models don't help a solo writer; offer a simple workflow instead.

## Workflow

### 1. Interview

Ask the questions in `references/intake-questions.md`, **three or four at a time**, in the order given. Don't dump all of them at once. Adapt follow-ups to what they say. If they answer with a vague "it's fine," ask for the last concrete example.

Stop interviewing when you can score every dimension with at least one specific example. Usually 10 to 14 questions.

### 2. Score

Load `references/maturity-model.md`. Score each of the six dimensions 1 to 5. Every score needs evidence: a direct quote or a specific detail from the interview. If you have no evidence, say "not enough signal" rather than guessing.

### 3. Find the bottleneck

The bottleneck is the lowest-scoring dimension that **upstream** dimensions depend on. Use the dependency order in the maturity model. A 2 in Strategy blocks everything; a 2 in Measurement only blocks learning. Don't just pick the lowest number.

Watch for the common false bottleneck: teams that score themselves low on Production capacity when the real constraint is Review and approval. If drafts sit in review more than a week, the bottleneck is governance, not writers.

### 4. Deliver

1. **Maturity snapshot.** A table: dimension, level (1–5), evidence (quote or detail), one-line read
2. **The bottleneck.** One paragraph. What it is, how you know, what it costs them right now in concrete terms
3. **Next two moves.** Specific, sized for the next 30 to 60 days, owned by a role. Not a roadmap.
4. **What not to do yet.** The tempting fix that won't work until the bottleneck moves (often "buy an AI writing tool" or "hire more writers")
5. **What would change the diagnosis.** The one fact you didn't get that might move the bottleneck

## Voice

A peer who's seen this before, not a consultant selling a phase two. Short sentences. No maturity-model jargon in the output beyond the level numbers; describe what each level looks like in their words.
