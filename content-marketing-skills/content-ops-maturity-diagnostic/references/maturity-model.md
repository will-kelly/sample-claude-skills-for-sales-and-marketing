# Content ops maturity model

Six dimensions, five levels each. Dimensions are listed in dependency order: problems higher in the list block everything below them.

## Dependency order

1. Strategy and audience
2. Governance and review
3. Production workflow
4. Findability and reuse
5. Tooling and AI use
6. Measurement and learning

## 1. Strategy and audience

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Content is requested ad hoc by whoever asks loudest. No documented audience. |
| 2 | An audience exists on paper. Nobody on the content team has talked to a customer in the last quarter. |
| 3 | Documented audiences and themes; requests are filtered against them most of the time. |
| 4 | Audience is validated with sales, support or customer conversations at least quarterly. Content plan ties to pipeline or adoption goals. |
| 5 | Strategy updates from evidence. The team kills topics that don't work. |

## 2. Governance and review

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | No defined approvers. Drafts go to "everyone" and stall. |
| 2 | Approvers exist but review is unbounded. More than a week in review is normal. |
| 3 | Named approvers per content type with expected turnaround. Exceptions still common. |
| 4 | Review SLAs mostly met. Legal and technical review scoped to what they actually need to check. |
| 5 | Risk-tiered review: low-risk content ships with one approver; high-risk gets the full chain. |

## 3. Production workflow

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Every piece is a one-off. Status lives in people's heads. |
| 2 | A tracker exists but isn't current. Briefs are optional. |
| 3 | Briefs required. Status visible to stakeholders. Templates for common types. |
| 4 | Stages and owners clear; work in progress limited; handoffs explicit. |
| 5 | Workflow measured and tuned. Cycle time known per content type. |

## 4. Findability and reuse

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Nobody can find last year's assets. Duplicates everywhere. |
| 2 | One repository exists, but it's not trusted. People ask in Slack instead. |
| 3 | Repository mostly trusted; naming and tagging inconsistent. |
| 4 | Assets tagged, owned and dated. Stale content flagged. Reuse is normal. |
| 5 | Single source of truth with lifecycle rules. Retired content is actually retired. |

## 5. Tooling and AI use

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Tools chosen individually. AI use is untracked and invisible. |
| 2 | Shared tools exist; AI used ad hoc with no guidelines. |
| 3 | AI guidelines written; some shared prompts or skills. |
| 4 | AI built into specific workflow steps with human review points defined. |
| 5 | AI steps measured for quality and time saved; the team retires what doesn't help. |

## 6. Measurement and learning

| Level | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 1 | Output counted (pieces shipped). Nothing else. |
| 2 | Traffic tracked. No connection to business outcomes. |
| 3 | Some content tied to pipeline, adoption or support deflection. |
| 4 | Regular review of what worked; decisions change because of it. |
| 5 | Measurement drives the plan. Low performers are cut or refreshed on a schedule. |

## Reading the scores

- A team at 4 in Tooling and 2 in Governance will produce faster drafts that sit in review longer. AI makes a governance bottleneck worse.
- A team at 2 in Strategy will see every downstream investment underperform. Fix strategy first even if it isn't the lowest number.
- Findability problems often show up as "we need more content." Check whether the content already exists before agreeing.
