# Results readout

Leaders and builders want different things from the same results. Sales leadership wants to know what changed for their reps and what decision they need to make. The wider forward-deployed team wants the pattern: what worked, what didn't, and what they can steal for their own team.

One document trying to serve both serves neither. This skill writes two.

## When to use

- After a build has a completed before-and-after ledger
- Monthly or quarterly roll-ups
- When a build gets killed (those readouts are often the most useful)

## Readout 1: leadership brief

Half a page. Lead with the rep's day, not the architecture.

1. **What changed.** One sentence about the work, in the team's language. "Reps now walk into first calls with an account brief that took 4 minutes instead of 25."
2. **The evidence.** Two or three numbers from the ledger, labeled measured, estimated or reported.
3. **What it cost.** Build time, ongoing ownership, anything the team had to change.
4. **The decision.** What you need from them: expand, fund the content fix, kill it, approve the graduation.
5. **What's next.** One line.

No model names, no agent diagrams, no "leveraging." If a VP of sales would have to ask what something means, cut it.

## Readout 2: pattern writeup

A page or two, for peers who build.

- **The workflow** and why it scored high in the audit
- **What we built**, at the level another builder could reproduce
- **What surprised us**, especially about how reps actually used it versus how we expected
- **What didn't work**, specifically. Iterations that failed and why.
- **Reusable parts**: prompts, skills, eval rubrics, runbook sections another team could lift
- **Where it would break** on a different team

## Rules

- Same numbers in both readouts. Never round up for leadership.
- If the result is mixed or a build was killed, say so in the first line. Credibility with leadership comes from the readouts where you didn't win.
- Credit the reps who tested it by name, with permission.
- Write it like you'd say it. If a sentence sounds like a press release, rewrite it.
