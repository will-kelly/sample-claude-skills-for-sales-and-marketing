# Content ops maturity model

Five levels, applied stage by stage. Most teams are at different levels in different stages, and that unevenness is the point of the diagnostic.

## The levels

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ad hoc | Happens when someone pushes. No owner, no pattern, no record. |
| 2 | Hero-dependent | Works because one person holds it together. Breaks when they're on vacation. |
| 3 | Defined | There's a known way to do it, written down, and most people follow it most of the time. |
| 4 | Managed | The process is measured. The team knows how long it takes and where it stalls, and adjusts. |
| 5 | Optimized | The process improves on purpose, with automation and AI where they've proven out, and humans where judgment matters. |

## Diagnostic questions by stage

### 1. Intake
- How does a request reach the content team? Count the channels.
- Who decides yes or no? Has anyone ever said no?
- What does a request look like when it arrives? A sentence in Slack, or a form with an audience and goal?
- Level 1 sign: "Whoever asks loudest gets it." Level 3 sign: one intake path and a named person who triages.

### 2. Planning
- How far ahead is the calendar real, not aspirational?
- Does every piece start with a brief? Who writes it?
- How do priorities connect to pipeline, product launches or business goals?
- Level 2 sign: the calendar lives in one person's head. Level 4 sign: planned versus shipped is tracked monthly.

### 3. Production
- Who writes, and how much is in-house versus freelance versus AI-assisted?
- Is there a style guide and voice guidance people actually use?
- How long from brief to first draft, typically?
- Level 1 sign: every draft starts from a blank page. Level 3 sign: templates and briefs make drafts predictable.

### 4. Review
- How many people review a typical piece? Who are they?
- How long does review take, from draft submitted to approved?
- Do reviewers know what they're reviewing for (accuracy, legal, brand)?
- Level 2 sign: "It goes to everyone and we wait." Level 4 sign: review time is tracked and reviewers have defined scopes.

### 5. Publishing
- Who can publish? How many steps between approved and live?
- Is SEO metadata, alt text, linking and tagging done every time?
- Level 1 sign: approved drafts sit for weeks waiting for someone with CMS access.

### 6. Distribution
- What happens after a piece goes live? Is that planned before it's written?
- Does sales know it exists?
- Level 1 sign: "We post it on LinkedIn once." Level 3 sign: every piece has a distribution plan in its brief.

### 7. Measurement
- What does the team report on? Who reads it?
- Can they name their three best-performing pieces and why?
- Does anything they learn change what gets planned?
- Level 2 sign: pageviews in a monthly deck nobody discusses. Level 4 sign: results feed back into planning.

### 8. Maintenance
- When was the last time an old piece was updated, merged or deleted?
- Is there an inventory of what exists?
- Who owns content after it ships?
- Level 1 sign: nobody knows how many pages there are. Level 3 sign: a scheduled refresh cycle for top content.

## Common constraint patterns

- **Intake at level 1** is the most common constraint. It shows up as "we can't produce fast enough" because the team is producing everything for everyone.
- **Review at level 2** shows up as missed launch dates and stale drafts.
- **Maintenance at level 1** shows up as "our SEO is declining" or "sales says our content is out of date."
- **Measurement at level 1–2** keeps every other stage from improving, because nobody can tell what worked.
