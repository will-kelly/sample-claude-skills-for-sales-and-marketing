# Request intake triage

Most overloaded content teams don't have a production problem. They have an intake problem. Requests arrive by Slack, email, hallway and executive drive-by, and every one gets a yes because nobody owns the no. The calendar fills with one-off assets for one person's deal, and the work that would actually move pipeline never starts.

This skill handles both halves: setting up intake so requests arrive in usable shape, and triaging what comes in.

## Mode 1: set up intake

Use when the team has no intake process or a broken one.

**Ask for**: who requests content today and through which channels, rough monthly request volume, team capacity in pieces per month, and the team's top goals for the quarter.

**Produce**:
1. **An intake form**, kept to seven fields or fewer. More than that and people route around it. Required fields: what's needed, who it's for, what they should do after reading it, the business reason, deadline and why, and who the subject matter expert is. Optional: links to source material.
2. **The triage rubric**, adapted from the default below to the team's goals.
3. **Service levels**: how fast requesters hear back (a decision within two business days is a good default) and what happens to declined requests.
4. **An announcement message** the content lead can send to requesters explaining the new process, in a friendly tone that doesn't sound like a bureaucracy.

## Mode 2: triage requests

Use when the user pastes one or more requests.

Score each request on the rubric:

| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goal fit | No link to current goals | Indirect | Directly supports a stated goal |
| Audience reach | One deal or person | One segment | Many buyers or the whole funnel |
| Reuse | Single use | Some reuse | Becomes a source for other assets |
| Existing coverage | We already have this | Partial | Real gap |
| Readiness | No SME, no source material | Some inputs | SME named, inputs ready |
| Deadline reality | Arbitrary or impossible | Tight but workable | Real date, workable |

**Score 9 or more**: accept. **6 to 8**: accept if there's capacity, or offer a smaller version. **Under 6**: decline or redirect.

Redirect options matter more than the score. A low-scoring request often has a cheaper answer: an existing asset, a section added to something planned, a sales email template instead of a white paper.

## Output format (triage)

| Request | Score | Decision | Reasoning (one line) |
|---|---|---|---|

Then for each request:
- **Accepted**: a starter brief with audience, goal, the one thing the reader should take away, format, SME, and due date.
- **Smaller version**: what you'd make instead and why it still meets the need.
- **Declined or redirected**: a short reply to the requester. Lead with what they can have, give the honest reason, and keep the door open.

## Rules

- Never decline without a redirect. A no with no alternative teaches people to go around the process.
- Executive requests get scored like everyone else's. The content lead can still choose to override, but the score makes the tradeoff visible.
- If more than a third of requests score under 6, tell the user. That's a signal the team's goals aren't communicated to the people asking.
- Keep requester replies human and brief. Nobody wants a rubric pasted into their DMs.
